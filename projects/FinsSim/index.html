Abstract

Underwater robot learning relies on simulators that integrate high-fidelity hydrodynamics, convenient learning interfaces, and a credible transition to real scenarios. In this work, we present FinsSim, a reality-aligned integrated simulation platform for Sim‑to‑Real underwater robot learning. FinsSim first constructs high-fidelity simulation with selectable backends to adapt to diverse requirements. To facilitate underwater robot research, it further offers standard control baselines, alongside with unified robot learning workflows. For reliable Sim-to-Real transfer, FinsSim adopts a multi-sensor fusion scheme to provide low-cost yet precise localization. Moreover, it implements calibrated thruster-hydrodynamics models and constrained wrench allocation algorithm. Bridging these modules by ROS~2, FinsSim establishes a complete Sim-to-Real transfer pipeline. Through matched simulations and experiments, it is demonstrated that reliable Sim-to-Real transfer of underwater robot control policies can be achieved with the FinsSim framework. Separate ablation studies also validate that the modules of FinsSim can address the pivotal issues of underwater Sim-to-Real from different aspects. Overall, this work aims to bridge the gap between theoretical research and practical applications, ultimately driving advancements in the field of underwater robotics.

Architecture

Simulation layer

Unity supplies scene-rich and sensor-oriented simulation, while Isaac Lab provides a GPU-native path for high-throughput state-based training. The vehicle can switch among calibrated Fossen, physics-mesh, and simplified Fossen dynamics.

Learning and control layer

PID, RL, IL, and MARL workflows share sensor, state, action, and evaluation contracts. Policies can operate through a six-channel body wrench or an eight-thruster interface.

Hardware layer

A fixed overhead camera, onboard AprilTags, pressure, and IMU measurements feed a fused state estimate. It’s the perception of the physical world. A hardware bridge and MCU turn bounded force commands into calibrated thruster execution.

FinsSim consists of a simulation platform and a hardware-connected physical platform, linked through ROS 2. The simulation platform supports Unity ML-Agents [10] and Isaac Lab [11] as simulation backends. It provides calibrated Fossen-form, simplified Fossen-form, and mesh-based hydrodynamic models, together with learning and control interfaces for policy training and evaluation. The physical platform combines a low-cost global localization system based on refractive AprilTags, pressure and IMU sensing, multi-sensor state estimation, and a ROS 2 hardware bridge that relays bounded control commands to the onboard MCU for calibrated eight-thruster actuation on FinsROV.

Low-cost Global Positioning

A fixed overhead camera observes two vehicle-mounted AprilTags [12] through a calibrated air-water interface. The camera ray is intersected with the interface, then refracted using Snell’s law before reconstruction at the pressure- and IMU-constrained tag-corner height. This provides horizontal position and yaw, while pressure supplies depth and the IMU constrains roll and pitch.

The resulting AprilTag measurement is fused by a position-velocity EKF and a separate yaw filter. Across 720 archived images at 170 surveyed pool locations, the refraction-aware measurement reaches 0.73 cm horizontal RMSE and 1.45 cm P95, compared with 5.74 cm and 11.41 cm for pinhole PnP.

Reality-aligned

FinsSim separates vehicle-specific calibration from task specification. The calibrated vehicle stack includes hydrodynamic coefficients, directional thrust curves, allocation geometry, and safety bounds; the same ROS 2 interface then connects simulation, controllers, and the FinsROV hardware bridge.

Hydrodynamic calibration

Single-axis trials identify effective mass or inertia, linear damping, quadratic damping, and constant bias for surge, sway, heave, and yaw. Roll and pitch use the corresponding restoring-moment model, and the resulting vehicle profile is transferred into the calibrated Fossen backend.

Thrust-curve calibration

Each thruster uses separately identified forward and reverse steady-state quadratic curves. The bridge maps requested force to target RPM, and the onboard MCU closes a feedforward-plus-PID speed loop using measured RPM feedback.

Bounded hardware execution

For body-wrench commands, a constrained allocator respects calibrated directional force limits and regularizes unnecessary thrust effort before sending the shared eight-channel command to Unity or the hardware bridge.

Learning Task Contracts

FinsSim specifies task-specific observations, normalized actions, reward functions, safety conditions, and evaluation metrics for underwater control. The reported policies use Proximal Policy Optimization [13] through Stable-Baselines3 [14]. PPO-Wrench6 and PPO-Thruster8 share task-specific observations and calibrated force limits while providing distinct action interfaces.

T1 / Station keeping

Hold a registered 4-DOF pose

Observation
16 dimensions: body-frame target displacement, continuous relative attitude, linear and angular velocity, and scalar distance-to-goal.
Action
Wrench6 emits a normalized six-axis body wrench in [-1, 1]. Thruster8 emits normalized commands for the eight canonical thrusters. Reported hardware results use Wrench6.
Reward
Position tracking and a sustained-hold bonus, attitude and angular-rate preference, low-energy smooth commands, and a penalty on task failure.
Evaluation
Eight fixed setpoints, 60 s per trial. Position and yaw RMSE are measured in the 40-60 s late-hold window; roll and pitch are safety guards.

T2 / Trajectory tracking

Follow a time-indexed 3D reference

Observation
30 dimensions: four future body-relative reference points, reference velocity and tangent, vehicle linear and angular velocity, control state, and trajectory encoding.
Action
The same Wrench6 and Thruster8 policy contracts are available. Hardware comparisons report Wrench6, which maps a normalized wrench to bounded thruster forces.
Reward
Path-tracking quality, uprightness and low yaw-rate preferences conditioned on tracking, low-energy actuation, and failure termination penalties.
Evaluation
Straight-line, broken-line, elliptical, and lemniscate references. The task tracks translation, with yaw, roll, and pitch maintained as stabilized axes.
